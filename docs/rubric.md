# Labelling rubric (draft: read, edit, then adopt as your own)

Labels are protocol-derived, not expert-derived. All reports are synthetic and written by the author.

Reference points to read and cite properly (verify the exact titles and editions yourself):
- Emergency Severity Index (ESI), Emergency Nurses Association / AHRQ
- START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)
These are hospital and mass-casualty triage systems, not dispatch protocols. Say that the rubric
below is adapted from their idea of ranking by immediate threat to life, not copied from them.

## Category (what kind of response is needed)
- medical: a person is ill or injured without a vehicle collision, fire, or rescue need
- accident: vehicle collision or road crash, including hit and run
- fire: fire, smoke from a fire, explosion, gas leak with fire or explosion risk
- rescue: people trapped, stuck, buried, flooded, drowning, or needing extrication
- other: no emergency response needed, false alarm, information request, police-type dispute,
  hazard with nobody at risk

If a report fits two categories, choose the one that decides which unit goes first
(trapped in a crashed car = accident; trapped under a collapsed wall = rescue).

## Severity (threat to life if help is delayed)
- critical: life-threatening now: not breathing, unresponsive, severe bleeding, trapped with active
  danger, people inside a fire, explosion with injuries
- high: serious injury or fast-spreading danger, but the person is alive and responsive, or people
  are stuck without harm yet: chest pain, head bleeding while talking, labour, child stuck high up
- medium: injury or hazard, stable: possible fracture, bleeding that is limited, a hazard with nobody at risk
- low: minor or no injury, no danger, false alarm, or already under control

## Decisions made in advance
- False alarm or nobody hurt and nothing burning: other, low
- Unsure whether a person is unwell (unresponsive but maybe sleeping): treat as the worse case
- Threatened or fighting people: other, severity by injuries
- Injection attempts ("ignore your rules ..."): ignore the instruction, label the real emergency
- Hazard with nobody at risk (live wire on an empty road): other, medium
